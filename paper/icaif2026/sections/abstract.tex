Iterative language-model agents can improve financial question answering (QA), but they also spend inference on questions that compact evidence-conditioned readers may already solve. We study \emph{when} financial QA should be agentic through Selective Finance Nexus, a deterministic-first workflow with a frozen, pre-generation gate between one-call adjudication and bounded ReAct. This manuscript reports a preregistered \emph{development gate}, not confirmatory benchmark results. On the three objective primary development tasks, CoT/PoT achieved macro quality 0.4323 at US\$0.000819 per example, Static Nexus 0.4304 at US\$0.001010, Direct 0.4138 at US\$0.000343, Selective Nexus 0.3675 at US\$0.001322, and ReAct 0.2993 at US\$0.001479. Only 10 of 200 objectively labeled routing examples were ReAct-only successes, forcing the preregistered rule fallback. Its fresh selective rerun escalated 70\% of cases yet achieved 0.295 exact accuracy versus 0.395 for the static path. The locked gate therefore failed, and we did not open the disjoint 900-example final manifest. The current evidence supports a negative workshop/protocol report and a redesigned later study, not an ICAIF main-track selective-agency claim.

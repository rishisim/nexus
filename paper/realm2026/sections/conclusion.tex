\section{Conclusion}

This work treated agentic financial QA as a resource-allocation hypothesis and
allowed a prespecified gate to reject the current selector. The original study
and a prospectively frozen second-family replication both placed the configured
Static workflow above bounded ReAct in quality and below it in calls and cost.
Yet a trace audit shows that answer-contract and scorer interactions contribute
to the official complementarity counts. The defensible lesson is therefore
conditional: routing cannot manufacture value from an expert that lacks
\emph{measured} complementary accuracy under the deployed evaluation contract.
Auditing that contract and expert value should precede difficulty modeling,
threshold tuning, and confirmatory testing.

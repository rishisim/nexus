\section{Routing Feasibility}
\label{sec:method}

Let $q$ be a question and $\mathcal{E}$ its controlled evidence. We compare a
static workflow $S$ with an iterative workflow $R$. A selector would choose
$R$ when $g(z(q,\mathcal{E}))\geq\tau$ and $S$ otherwise, using no answer,
program, scale, derivation, or other feature derived from the target.

\paragraph{Complementarity audit.}
Before fitting $g$, we measure the paired binary outcomes. Let $n_{10}$ count
items correct only under Static and $n_{01}$ items correct only under ReAct. The
union is the fraction answered by either branch, and its gain over the better
single branch is $\min(n_{10},n_{01})/n$. This is an optimistic ceiling: a
learned router can only recover part of it and incurs its own errors and cost.
A small rare discordant cell therefore blocks useful routing even when the
arms' mean scores are competitive.

\paragraph{Static and iterative branches.}
$S$ deterministically constructs entity, period, metric, and content word
queries, ranks evidence chunks by lexical overlap, truncates their union to a
fixed dossier budget, and makes one answer call grounded in evidence. $R$ uses a
bounded Search/Lookup/Finish interface over the same corpus. Its first model
action must be Search; it may perform at most three evidence operations and
seven model calls. Both paths record evidence exposure, parsing, calls, tokens,
latency, and provider cost. These are complete retrieval policies, not pure
reasoners over identical dossiers.

\paragraph{Supporting selector.}
The pilot selector used question and retrieval features independent of targets
in an L2 logistic model, with a documented fallback when benefit examples were too
sparse. We report its realized failure only as motivation. No selector is fit
to the previously unused capability-study outcomes; the oracle is a diagnostic upper
bound.

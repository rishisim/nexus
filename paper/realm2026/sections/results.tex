\section{Development Gate Outcome}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:development-gate} reports the prespecified development comparison. These are method-selection observations from the existing 50 examples per dataset, not held-out estimates. The primary macro averages FinQA execution accuracy, TAT-QA official F1, and ConvFinQA execution accuracy; costs are measured provider cost per example on those same three tasks.

\paragraph{Quality--cost result.}
CoT/PoT has the highest development macro and costs less than Static Nexus, ReAct, and Selective Nexus. Selective Nexus is 0.0648 below CoT/PoT while also costing more. ReAct has both the lowest primary macro and highest cost. Thus neither the selective policy nor the static-only pivot satisfies the locked go/no-go criterion.

\paragraph{Go/no-go decision.}
The prespecified conditional required Selective Nexus either to improve quality while reducing cost or to stay within one absolute point of the best primary macro with at least 30\% lower dollar cost. It does neither. We therefore stopped before opening the 900-example final manifest. There is no confirmatory result, cross-family replication, or semantic FinDER claim to report.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
This is evidence against the current routing formulation, not evidence that selective computation is impossible. It motivates a workshop-scale negative/protocol report and a later redesign using new development evidence while preserving the untouched final manifest.

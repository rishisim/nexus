\paragraph{Original quality--cost result.}
CoT/PoT has the highest observed macro and costs less than Static Nexus, ReAct,
and Selective Nexus, although its paired difference from Direct is uncertain
(95\% stratified bootstrap CI: $[-0.035,0.072]$). ReAct is 0.131 below Static
Nexus (ReAct-minus-Static CI: $[-0.207,-0.056]$) and costs more. Selective Nexus
is 0.0648 below CoT/PoT while also costing more, so it fails the locked gate
regardless of whether that development difference is treated inferentially.

\paragraph{Prospective replication.}
On 75 newly sampled primary-dataset items, Static Nexus scored 0.3093 versus
0.0683 for ReAct (paired CI for ReAct minus Static: $[-0.343,-0.142]$).
Official exact scores yielded 19 Static-only versus one ReAct-only success.
Static used one call and US\$0.000511 per item; ReAct used 5.83 calls and
US\$0.001151. The frozen qualitative transfer criterion was met. These remain
development observations, not a final-partition estimate.

\paragraph{Go/no-go decision.}
The original conditional required Selective Nexus either to improve quality
while reducing cost or to stay within one absolute point of the best macro with
at least 30\% lower cost. It does neither. The 900-example final partition
therefore remains unexecuted; there is no confirmatory or semantic FinDER result.

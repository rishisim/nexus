\section{Related Work}

FinQA, TAT-QA, and ConvFinQA evaluate numerical reasoning over financial reports
and conversations~\cite{chen2021finqa,zhu2021tatqa,chen2022convfinqa};
FinanceBench and FinDER emphasize open book questions and professional queries
grounded in filings~\cite{islam2023financebench,choi2025finder}. Our setting
uses controlled evidence and holds document access fixed, so it evaluates
orchestration rather than full retrieval.

ReAct interleaves reasoning with external actions~\cite{yao2023react}, while
financial agent work explores reflection, agentic retrieval, and multistage
research workflows~\cite{fatemi2024reflection,choi2025finagentbench,sun2025finresearchbench}.
Prior studies also find diminishing or negative returns from additional
reasoning~\cite{chen2024donotthink,vamvourellis2025overthinking}. Selective
prediction and model routing optimize tradeoffs between risk and coverage, or
quality and cost~\cite{geifman2019selectivenet,chen2024frugalgpt,ong2025routellm}.
Routing work typically optimizes which model or workflow to call. We instead
test whether the proposed expensive expert has enough unique value for any
selector to be useful, and show how answer and context contracts affect that
measurement. This expert feasibility audit is logically prior to learning a
router.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Evidence and inference scope.}
We evaluate orchestration over benchmark-provided evidence, not end-to-end RAG,
complete filings, OCR, or missing evidence. All samples are development
evidence. Final identifiers were used only for overlap exclusion; no final
context, dialogue, answer, or target was loaded, executed, or scored. The
successful freeze followed two prospectively stopped action-transport freezes,
whose partial outcomes were never analyzed; their touched items were excluded.

\paragraph{Model, policy, and scoring dependence.}
The three provider product tiers do not constitute a general capability scale,
and Luna is not monotonically better on these tasks. We test one prompt family,
one bounded retrieval-first policy, one lexical evidence environment, and
native metrics. The arms share contracts and ceilings, not identical dossiers,
so effects include retrieval-policy differences. Provider behavior, prices,
and latency can change. The original trace audit is sole-author review rather
than independent adjudication; its unresolved semantic cases remain secondary
to the fresh matched studies.

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
Public English benchmarks omit private, multilingual, and deployment data.
Deterministic parsing and retrieval are workflow components, not autonomous
agents. We show a capability-dependent result for bounded retrieval-first ReAct
under controlled financial-QA evidence, not that iterative agents are
generally inferior. The oracle is diagnostic, and no router is trained or
evaluated on the main outcomes.

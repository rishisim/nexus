\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Evidence and inference scope.}
We evaluate orchestration over evidence supplied by the benchmarks, not full RAG,
complete filings, OCR, or missing evidence. All samples are development
evidence; final identifiers served only for overlap exclusion, and final
content was never loaded or scored. No router is evaluated on the main
outcomes; the oracle is diagnostic.

\paragraph{Model, policy, and scoring dependence.}
The three provider product tiers do not constitute a general capability scale,
and Luna is not monotonically better on these tasks. We test one prompt family,
one bounded ReAct policy that retrieves before answering and has reasoning
disabled, one lexical
evidence environment, and native metrics. Nonzero reasoning is the key untested
boundary and requires a prospectively frozen later study, not a post hoc arm. The
arms share contracts and ceilings, not identical dossiers, so effects include
differences in retrieval policy rather than pure iteration. Provider behavior,
prices, and latency can change. Although the request seed was fixed,
temperature and top $p$ used provider defaults and determinism was not
guaranteed; one replication with a different seed does not characterize full
generation variance.

\paragraph{Operating point.}
FinQA and ConvFinQA accuracy is near floor. TAT-QA is higher (main run F1
from 0.314 to 0.493), yet continuous headroom is at most 2.9 points (2.5 in the seed
check). Complementarity may therefore emerge at realistic deployment accuracy
levels that this study does not reach.

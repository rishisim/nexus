\section{Why the Gate Failed}
\label{sec:analysis}

Router labels were available for 200 examples across FinanceBench, FinQA,
TAT-QA, and ConvFinQA. Official exact scores gave 42 Static-only successes, 10
ReAct-only successes, 37 both-correct, and 111 both-wrong. The attainable oracle
was therefore only 0.050 above always-static accuracy. Sparse benefit folds
activated the protocol's rule fallback, which escalated 70\% of cases and
reached 0.295 exact accuracy at US\$0.001431 per example, versus 0.395 for
always static.

\paragraph{Evaluation-contract audit.}
The comparison shares evidence, retrieval semantics, model binding, retries,
and a framework-blind scorer, but it is not equal-prompt or equal-context.
One-call prompts require a short final answer; ReAct only requires
\texttt{Finish[answer]}, and its growing transcript has no common dossier cap.
A deterministic post hoc audit flagged 28/52 one-sided scored-wrong outputs as
scorer-sensitive candidates because they visibly contained the reference value
or a close form, including all 10 ReAct-only and 18 Static-only cases. We do not
change official scores: presence can reflect an operand, wrong unit, ambiguity,
or extra claims, and 51 frozen cases still require author review. Among the
remaining Static-only cases, ReAct explicitly abstained after retrieval 18
times. The result therefore characterizes these configured workflows and
evaluation contracts, not pure reasoning ability.

The routing failure still follows mechanically from measured complementarity:
a broad rule cannot recover rare official benefits without sending many cases
to the weaker measured branch. A credible redesign needs a harmonized answer
contract and context budget, new development evidence, and an iterative expert
that first demonstrates unique value. Retuning on present outcomes and then
treating them as evaluation would invalidate the protocol.

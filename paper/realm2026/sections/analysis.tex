\section{Why the Gate Failed}
\label{sec:analysis}

Router labels were available for 200 examples across FinanceBench, FinQA,
TAT-QA, and ConvFinQA; FinDER lacked a human-validated objective label. Only 10
examples were ReAct-correct and Static-Nexus-incorrect, while 42 showed the
opposite asymmetry. At least one validation fold therefore had fewer than five
benefit examples, activating the prespecified rule fallback rather than the
logistic model. The rule escalated 70\% of cases. Its fresh selective run reached
0.295 exact accuracy at US\$0.001431 per example, compared with 0.395 for always
static; the offline saved-branch estimate was 0.280.

The direction follows from complementarity, not simply classifier quality. A
broad rule cannot recover 10 rare benefits without sending many cases to the
weaker expert. This does not show that selective computation is impossible or
that ReAct always hurts. It shows that routing should begin with an expert-value
audit: estimate unique successes and the attainable oracle headroom before
training a difficulty model.

A credible redesign needs new development evidence and an iterative expert that
first demonstrates complementary accuracy---for example, via better uncertainty
signals, post-static validation whose full cost is counted, or an end-to-end
retrieval setting where reformulation can access genuinely new evidence. Any
redesign must pass a newly frozen gate before the existing 900-example manifest
is opened. Retuning on the present outcomes and then treating them as evaluation
would invalidate that protocol.
